# Episodes 2–4 — generation and composition prompts

These are **production prompts**, not permission to invent new canon. Generate language-neutral art only. Match the approved Episode 1 character reference and inking/cel-shading style.

## Verified location anchors

- **Stawa Młyny:** official Świnoujście sources describe it as a 10 m navigational beacon at the end of the Western Breakwater, built in 1873–1874 and still serving navigation. It has the distinctive white cylindrical “windmill” form and sails.
  - https://www.swinoujscie.pl/artykul/stawa-mlyny-oficjalnie-zabytkiem
  - https://visit.swinoujscie.pl/en/
- **Park Zdrojowy:** official city history describes geometric alleys and circular spaces developed around the turn of the 19th century; Peter Joseph Lenné adapted the park plan in 1826.
  - https://www.swinoujscie.pl/artykul/park-zdrojowy
- **Fort Anioła:** official tourism material describes a mid-19th-century three-storey rotunda with terrace/observation tower, inspired by Castel Sant’Angelo in Rome.
  - https://visit.swinoujscie.pl/aktualnosci/5-pomyslow-na-rowerowa-wycieczke/

Do not copy a reference photograph composition. Use references only to keep landmark geometry credible.

## Global visual prompt

Expressive hand-inked family adventure graphic novel, cinematic staging, textured cel shading, believable hands and warm natural faces, teal Baltic water, warm sand/stone, restrained coral accents. Portrait 4:7 panel, ideally 1024×1792 or larger. Same Maja/Maks/Lea/Frytka designs as Episode 1. No text, no logos, no speech balloons, no fake signage. Preserve deliberate empty zones for DOM speech balloons. Contemporary family mystery, not fantasy history.

# Episode 2 — The Mill That Does Not Mill

### Panel 2.1 — Not a mill
Low-angle portrait view on the broad Western Breakwater with the real Stawa Młyny rising centrally but not filling the sky: white cylindrical navigational beacon, dark cap/base details and four windmill-style sails. Maja, Maks and Lea remain safely on the public walking surface in the lower third. Maks points with excited “windmill!” certainty; Maja gives a corrective, amused look; Lea studies the structure rather than posing. Keep two clean sky pockets in upper left and upper right for speech. Do not make it a rustic grain mill. No treasure chest.

Protected visual core: beacon centre and character faces/hands in lower middle.

### Panel 2.2 — Sail angle
Close, tactile investigation rather than another alignment shot. The translucent card is held at chest height in the lower-middle foreground; one abstract symbol echoes the angular position of the beacon sails through reflection/light, not through a perfect overlay. Lea notices the geometry; Maks reacts triumphantly; Maja remains skeptical. Stawa Młyny is recognisable but partially out of focus behind them. Reserve upper-left and upper-right negative space and a low clean strip for dialogue.

Protected visual core: translucent card, reflected/angular clue, hands, three faces.

### Panel 2.3 — Line along the breakwater
Over-shoulder portrait composition: the card occupies lower-left foreground and now shows a thin fictional line extending visually in the same direction as the Western Breakwater. The family looks along the safe walking route; sea spray is visible farther ahead, but the card has not been hit yet. Beacon sits behind/off-centre so this is not another landmark portrait. Reserve a clean top band and a clean lower-right strip.

Final visual question: the drawn line appears to stop near where wind-blown water could reach the card.

# Episode 3 — Ink from the Waves

### Panel 3.1 — Water reveal
Dynamic but safe portrait shot on the broad breakwater. A gust throws sea spray across the scene and a few droplets reach the translucent waterproof card held at chest height; nobody leans over or enters the water. Maks reacts as if the sea itself is drawing; Lea immediately studies a newly visible hidden print. Keep the new print abstract and incomplete: do not reveal Fort Anioła or later answers. Reserve two quiet upper corners.

Protected visual core: droplets contacting card, hands, newly revealed mark, faces.

### Panel 3.2 — Too-early treasure
Medium reaction/detail shot. Maks celebrates too early with comic confidence; Lea points out that the revealed mark resembles a park/leaf-path motif rather than treasure. Maja can be present as a reaction but should not dominate. The card remains central, with the new symbol clearly different from Episode 2’s sail-angle clue. Reserve top-left/top-right speech zones.

Protected visual core: park-like symbol, card, Lea’s pointing hand, expressions.

### Panel 3.3 — Inland
Transition composition from coast toward city: family walking away from the immediate sea along a permitted public route, the card in foreground showing the new fictional line bending inland. Do not invent a literal official trail. Suggest greenery ahead rather than showing the full park answer. Maks looks from the many possible directions back to the card. Reserve upper two pockets and lower band.

Final visual question: several possible paths/urban directions ahead, only one implied by the card.

# Episode 4 — Park as Map

### Panel 4.1 — Search the pattern
Wide-feeling portrait panel under the mature tree canopy of Park Zdrojowy. Branching public paths and geometric/circular planning cues are visible without claiming survey-level cartographic accuracy. Lea compares the transparent layer with a public information/map board or the visible path pattern; Maks realises they must read the whole layout, not hunt one object. Keep upper left/right quiet.

Protected visual core: path junction, map/card, character faces/hands.

### Panel 4.2 — Frytka chooses crumbs
Comic reaction panel. Frytka lands confidently by the wrong bench because of crumbs while Maks points as if the gull solved the clue. Maja immediately understands the mistake; Lea notices the matching path on the opposite side. Keep the gull natural and uncostumed. No feeding action needs to be shown. Reserve upper corners and lower strip.

Protected visual core: Frytka/bench, faces, Lea’s direction cue.

### Panel 4.3 — Round shape
High, graphic-but-believable view of a path junction/public plan plus the translucent fictional layer. The pattern resolves into a **round/rotunda-like clue** associated with Fort Anioła, but do not show a full detailed fort portrait yet—that belongs to Episode 5. Lea recognises the shape; Maks is certain; Maja’s expression reconnects the clue to the unknown sender. Reserve top-left/top-right and lower band.

Final visual question: the round fort clue is clear, but the sender’s intent is not.
